# Legal MD teacher coverage pilot (2026-10-05)

Related: #88, #83. The next capacity comparison needs legal, environment-bound
training data; the old ten-deck dataset cannot be relabeled. Before generating
a formal train/held-out split, measure coverage and real-shuffle transfer of
explicit opening targets across all 20 `md-2026-09` meta decks.

## Frozen development protocol

- Eight hands per deck, indices 0–7, base seed and solver seed `2026100501`.
- 3,000 ms per hand, one solver thread, four workers, one line, 30 s timeout,
  no fire variants. These are development hands, never formal held-out hands.
- Targets: `md-bc-targets-2026-10-05.json` next to this report. Each is an
  archetype-specific opening milestone present in the legal deck, not a claim
  of an optimal board or effective interruption count. Odion targets its main
  monster; Sky Striker targets Shizuku. Do not treat those different milestones
  as equal strength or compare decks' strength by solve rate.
- Independently replay every solved line in the pseudo-shuffle setup. Also
  follow every solved line with pseudo-shuffle cleared, semantically matching
  actions, through the start of turn 2 (`build.first_turn.replay_line`). Report
  target retention and exact line-following success separately.
- Record unsolved, unverified and error hands in the denominator; distinguish
  line mismatch under shuffle from engine failure. No seed selection or
  increasing budgets in this pilot after inspecting its results.
- This recipe can advance to formal all-deck data only with zero engine/replay
  errors, at least 4/8 solved hands **in every deck**, and >=90% of solved lines
  following without mismatch and retaining their targets under real shuffling.
  Passing is a data-generation gate, not evidence of competitive play.
- If coverage/transfer fails, diagnose teacher generation before large-network
  training. Any revised recipe gets a separate development run. Formal held-out
  hands must remain unseen while choosing targets/budgets.

## Resume contract repair

`solve_openings.py` binds a sidecar manifest to complete environment identity,
ordered deck contents, targets/fire variants, seeds/budgets/threads/line count/
timeout, and hashes of actual solver/native/Python/scripts/card data. It refuses
unmarked old data and mismatched manifests before repairing a partial tail.
Hand ranges and worker count may change; output access is locked for the entire
run. Summaries and exit status include old records, so a zero-work resume cannot
hide earlier failures. A partially written hand's missing fire records use its
persisted plain line instead of a newly sampled search result.

Wall-clock search budgets remain hardware/load dependent, even with a fixed
seed and one thread. The manifest prevents incompatible mixing; it does not
promise byte-identical independent searches. Freeze the resulting files for
capacity comparisons. Inputs must remain unchanged during a running batch.
