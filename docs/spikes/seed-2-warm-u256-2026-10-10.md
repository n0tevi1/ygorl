## The 33-turn tail: two independently tested action candidates

The longest primary-panel game is Greedy game 127: turn-33 win, 1,067 decisions,
LP 7250/−3450. It has zero cancels and at most five same-choice repetitions, so
its duration cannot simply be attributed to a long selection loop.

At decision 3, on turn 1 with both fields empty and both players at 8000 LP, the
candidate uses Ash on its own Mulcharmy Purulia activation. Self-Ash probability
is **9.1776%**, versus 90.8224% for passing: this is a sampled low-probability
action, unlike the near-deterministic bad material choices in earlier cases.
At decision 228 it attacks a public 550-ATK monster with 100-ATK Lunalight Black
Sheep, losing the monster and 450 LP; the attack-command probability is **92.548%**.

The full original policy is independently reproduced, including all actions,
responses and result. Two separate single-action interventions then let both
original agents continue naturally:

| Intervention | Outcome | Turns | Decisions | Candidate terminal LP |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original sampled policy | win | 33 | 1067 | 7250 |
| Pass instead of self-Ash at decision 3 | win | 17 | 633 | 5800 |
| Enter main phase 2 instead of attacking at 228 | win | 17 | 730 | 8000 |

All three full runs have healthy exact cold replays. These selected-case outcomes
show useful alternatives in a long game, not that either action is its sole cause
or that either intervention generalizes. They do not justify banning all self-
negation or low-ATK attacks: resource release, graveyard value, mandatory effects
and subsequent policy decisions can change their value. In particular, the two
interventions are separate branches, not a tested combined policy.
