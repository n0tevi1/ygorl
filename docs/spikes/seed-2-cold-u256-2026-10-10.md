## Two candidate loops independently reproduced

Both complete policy runs reproduce original results, responses and recorded steps.

* Old-256×2 game 123: 20 material cancels at decisions 228–266, always the same model
  input and **91.8922%** cancel probability. It samples a non-cancel at 268 without
  reaching the cap, then wins at turn 5 / 285 decisions.
* Old-256×2 game 4: 32 target cancels at 165–227, unchanged input and **97.2062%**
  cancel probability. At 229 the existing guard masks cancel and the policy exits;
  it eventually loses at turn 20 / 819 decisions. This is Ecclesia's second target
  selection, not a failed Lua resolution.

Game 123 exposes more than a missing retry counter: across 21 material windows the
policy selected **five different Fusion goals**, yet all legacy material-window
inputs were equal. The chosen goal is player-known information. With the existing
opt-in selection-history encoding, native/reference tensors agree throughout both
full games, responses remain identical, and the pre-exit windows become 20 and 32
distinct inputs respectively. The latest selection token distinguishes all five
Fusion goals. This verifies representation, not learning or a trained policy gain;
the frozen formal checkpoints still use the legacy input schema.

Removing only complete cancel/reselect cycles while keeping the original action
suffix saves 40/64 decisions, with exactly unchanged winner, turn count and LP.
Keeping one cancel saves 38/62. These fixed-suffix probes isolate wasted decisions.
Natural sampled-policy budget-1 runs instead produce:

| Selected game | Original | Natural budget 1 |
|---|---|---|
| 123 | win, 5 turns, 285 decisions, 20 cancels | win, 13 turns, 538 decisions, 2 cancels |
| 4 | loss, 20 turns, 819 decisions, 32 cancels | win, 21 turns, 704 decisions, 1 cancel |

Both interventions are healthy and cold-replay exactly. Their changed outcomes are
not evidence of population strength or monotonic shortening. In game 4, restoring
only the candidate RNG at the matching post-loop state restores the complete original
suffix and loss, with 62 fewer decisions. In game 123, that hypothesis **fails**:
equal observation tensors conceal a different pending Fusion goal (baseline 41373230,
restricted 44146295). Restoring RNG alone still summons a different monster. The
failed first diagnostic and corrected analysis are retained. Equal tensors do not
establish equal engine state; a counterfactual must check the selected goal too.

Game 4 also attacks with 800 ATK into visible 1800 DEF at decision 552, losing 1000
LP at an attack probability of 99.11%. Its earlier 400-LP loss at 350 attacked a
face-down target; it must not be labeled as knowing that hidden DEF in advance.
These remain behavior investigation signals, not universal action prohibitions.
