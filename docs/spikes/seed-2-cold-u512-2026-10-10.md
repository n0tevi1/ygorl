## Old-256×2 game 205: completing a selected goal versus restarting selection

The candidate, engine player 1, wins at turn 10 / 662 decisions after 15 material
cancels. An independent full policy run reproduces every original action and
response, and every diagnostic run cold-replays exactly with zero engine errors.
After activating Polymerization in main phase 2, Vision HERO Trinity (46759931) is
the only selectable Fusion goal. All three available HERO monsters are required.

The model repeatedly avoids completing that set: at an empty material window
cancel has probability 57.372%; after partial selections, undo can exceed 98%.
It eventually samples the remaining material at decision 553 (6.206% probability),
finishes at 554, and proceeds to placement at 555. This is not the idle-menu
reentry mechanism in [the prior summon audit](summon-reentry-2026-10-10.md).

The force-material branch develops another cancellation sequence later. Thus
neither completing this procedure nor cancel suppression is a general learned
behavior fix. The decline result also does not justify banning the activation.
These are selected-case continuations under fallible policies, not optimal-action
labels or win-rate estimates.

The longer natural continuations were investigated rather than counted as harm
from faster selection. At matching post-Fusion placement, all input tensors and
player/turn/phase/LP agree. Restoring **only the candidate sampling generator**
then reproduces the entire original suffix for both interventions, including the
same turn-10 win and LP. Budget 1 uses **614 decisions (48 fewer)**; direct material
completion uses **606 (56 fewer)**. This isolates wasted selection work and the
effect of shifting subsequent random draws in this case. Equal tensors alone
would not establish this; the complete suffix equality is separately verified.

## Initial-128×2 game 65: selected-goal aliasing and adverse battle candidates

The candidate wins at turn 11 / 655 decisions with 13 cancels, a maximum of nine
consecutive cancels, and no engine errors. Independent policy replay matches the
original transcript. Across material windows 527/529/531/533/535 it selected
**three different Fusion goals** (89851827, 79755671, 33760966), yet the legacy
model inputs are identical. The chosen goal is known to the player but absent
from that representation.

The existing opt-in selection-history encoding distinguishes those goals. A full
655-decision replay verifies native/reference tensor and response parity in both
legacy and history modes; the latest selection token preserves all three goal
identities. This is a representation result, not proof that enabling the feature
in an untrained legacy checkpoint improves play. Earlier history/credit pilots
remain negative or inconclusive; they are not overwritten by this example.

The later window at 548–564 repeats cancellation at **76.853%** probability before
sampling an exit. Natural budget 1 still wins, but takes **14 turns / 924 decisions**
versus 11/655. It cannot be called a general shortening improvement.

The battle review also verifies two attacks into an already public 1800-ATK
monster, with 1300- and 1500-ATK attackers, losing 500 and 300 LP and both monsters.
Attack-command probabilities are **98.308%** and **97.372%**. These are clear
adverse immediate outcomes, but graveyard value and later play must still be
considered. Forcing main phase 2 before those attacks produces a turn-11 loss in
650 decisions. That single policy continuation neither proves the attacks good
nor supports a blanket attack ban. Keep these as battle/continuation challenge
cases, not automatic error labels.

## Historical-RL game 211: the selection-step cap can be repeatedly restarted

The candidate loses at turn 18 / 819 decisions, with six cancels and a 144-decision
interval without game-progress events. The original full policy transcript is
independently reproduced without engine errors. At decisions 538 and 572 the
selection-step counter reaches 32; the model cancels, returns to Fusion-goal
selection, and the per-selection step counter resets. The cancellation counter
correctly remains 5 and then 6. The model eventually completes materials at
605–606. No Python/native mismatch or new engine bug is established.

Candidate budget 1 yields a turn-18 loss in **771 decisions**, LP −1200/3700
instead of −500/3700. It saves work on this selected deal but does not rescue the
game, and its changed terminal LP must not be omitted.

## Decisions

Continue the registered warm run and complete the matched three-seed review.
Preserve the improved cold checkpoint and all behavioral counterexamples. Do not
replace terminal reward with raw turn minimization, ban cancellation globally,
or claim that fewer cancels implies stronger play.

The independent [4,096-game inference confirmation](policy-inference-broad-results-2026-10-10.md)
also fails to establish a primary win-rate gain from argmax + budget 1 and contains
one decision-limit failure. Keep default sampling. The next learning validation
must jointly cover selected-goal identity, successful material completion, valid
revision, and whole-game continuation; simply repeating the previously negative
terminal-credit experiment is not justified.
